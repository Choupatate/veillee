Help

A quick guide to what's here and how to use it. Nothing below is required reading — most of it you'll never need, and everything is forgiving: nothing here can be lost by accident.

Writing a memory

A Story is a full dated entry — a title, the day it happened, and as much writing and as many photos as you like. An Instant is quicker: a photo and one line, for the small moments that don't need a whole story written about them.

Not ready to publish something? Save it as a draft — only you (and other family members writing in the same book) can see it until you come back and finish it.

Want to write something for later, rather than now? A sealed letter stays hidden from the timeline — even from you — until the "open on" date you set for it arrives.

If a story marks a real first — first steps, first word, first day of school — give it a short milestone label in the editor. It'll show up on the Firsts page, a chronological list of every first that's happened.

The cast — people and the family tree

Add family and friends as people, so you can tag who was in a story and see the ones they appear in. Link parents and partners between people to build the family tree, which works out relationships ("your great-aunt") on its own — you never type a kinship word yourself.

A person's page can also record their birthday, a death, and wedding/PACS dates. Recorded dates quietly surface on the timeline the day they land (a birthday banner, an anniversary), and the Almanac lists every one of them, month by month, like a family record book.

Growing up

If you've set the child's birth date (an app setting, ask whoever set up Storybook), the Growing up page picks the story photo nearest each birthday, so you can watch them grow year by year in one glance.

Reading it back

The timeline is the everyday view — everything in order, newest at the top. Open a page at random for a surprise from the past, or check what happened on this day in an earlier year (shown right on the timeline when there's a match).

Read as a book turns every story into real book pages, one flowing read from the beginning — good for printing or saving as a PDF. Download as EPUB does the same for an e-reader (Kindle, Apple Books, calibre) instead.

Voice memos

Any story can have a short voice recording attached — a child's own voice, or someone telling the story out loud, alongside the written version. Recording needs a secure connection (HTTPS or localhost); ask whoever set up Storybook if it doesn't seem to work.

Family accounts

This book uses individual accounts rather than one shared password. Manage your own password and generate one-off write links — letting someone add a single story without an account of their own — from Account in the nav.

Keeping it safe

Everything here is plain files on disk — no cloud, no lock-in. Download everything (.zip) from the timeline's footer whenever you want a full copy; Import a backup restores one. If in doubt, ask whoever hosts this Storybook how often the underlying files are backed up.

Credit where it's due

Storybook is built on software other people wrote and gave away. Licences lists all of it, with the terms each one is offered under.
